At nearly 8,000 feet, Aspen has always asked something of its visitors' lungs. Now, several of the town's wellness properties are turning that challenge into a service category, building out treatment menus specifically designed around altitude adjustment and recovery.

Alpenglow Wellness House added a supplemental oxygen lounge to its spa menu this fall, alongside compression therapy and IV hydration services aimed at travelers arriving from sea level. General manager Peter Vance says the altitude-specific menu came directly from guest requests. "We kept hearing the same complaints — headaches, fatigue, trouble sleeping the first night," he said. "So we built a menu that treats altitude adjustment as its own category, not an afterthought."

## From Amenity to Revenue Line

What began as a complimentary oxygen bar for hotel guests has grown into a standalone revenue line, with day visitors booking altitude recovery packages even when they aren't staying at the property. Vance says the oxygen lounge and IV hydration bookings have become one of the property's fastest-growing service categories over the past two seasons.

The shift has prompted a few competing properties in the area to add similar offerings, though Vance says Alpenglow's early entry into the space gave it a head start on staff training and treatment protocols. The property now employs a nurse practitioner on-site several days a week to oversee IV therapy services, a staffing change Vance says was necessary once demand moved beyond a simple oxygen bar.

## A Broader Mountain Wellness Pattern

Altitude recovery treatments have become increasingly common across Colorado's mountain towns, with similar offerings appearing in Vail, Telluride, and Steamboat Springs over the past couple of years. Industry observers point to a broader wellness tourism trend where visitors expect destination-specific treatments rather than generic spa menus.

Vance says Alpenglow is now testing a pre-arrival consultation service, where guests fill out a health questionnaire before traveling so staff can recommend a recovery package before they even check in.
