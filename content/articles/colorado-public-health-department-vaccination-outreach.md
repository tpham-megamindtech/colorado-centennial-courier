Colorado's state public health department has expanded vaccination outreach programs, specifically targeting communities that have historically shown lower routine childhood and seasonal vaccination rates compared to statewide averages.

Public health outreach director Talia Mensah says the expanded program uses community health workers embedded directly within targeted communities, rather than relying solely on general public awareness campaigns, an approach informed by research showing that trusted community relationships are often more effective than broad messaging campaigns at addressing vaccine hesitancy. "General public health messaging reaches some people," Mensah said. "But building trust in specific communities usually requires people from those communities doing that outreach work directly."

## Building Community-Based Outreach Infrastructure

The expanded program has hired and trained community health workers specifically recruited from within the targeted communities, rather than deploying general public health staff without existing community relationships. These community health workers receive training in vaccination information and communication techniques, then work within their own communities to address specific questions and concerns residents may have about vaccination.

Public health officials say this community-based model requires more time and resources to build than broad public messaging campaigns, but say early data suggests improved vaccination rate trends in communities where the program has been active longest, compared to communities where outreach has only recently begun.

## Addressing Root Causes of Vaccine Hesitancy

Public health researchers involved in designing the program's approach emphasize that vaccine hesitancy often stems from a complex mix of factors, including historical experiences with the healthcare system, specific misinformation exposure, and practical access barriers, rather than a single uniform cause across all under-vaccinated communities. The program's community health worker model is designed to identify and address the specific factors relevant to each community, rather than applying a uniform outreach approach statewide.

State health officials say they're tracking vaccination rate data closely across program communities, viewing sustained improvement in routine vaccination rates as the clearest measure of the expanded outreach program's success, with plans to expand the community health worker model to additional communities if current results continue to show positive trends.
