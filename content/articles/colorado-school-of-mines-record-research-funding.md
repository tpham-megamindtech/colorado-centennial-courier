The Colorado School of Mines has reported record annual external research funding, driven largely by growing federal grants supporting energy and advanced materials science research at the Golden-based university, which has built a national reputation in these specialized research areas.

University research office officials attribute the funding growth to a combination of factors, including expanded federal research priorities around energy transition technologies and the university's growing track record of successful research outcomes that has strengthened its competitiveness for federal grant funding. "Success tends to compound in research funding," said research office spokesperson Diane Halloway. "A strong track record makes the next grant application more competitive."

## Energy Research Drives Funding Growth

A significant share of the new research funding supports projects related to critical minerals processing, energy storage technology, and geothermal energy research, areas where Mines has built particular research strength given its historic focus on earth sciences and engineering. Federal research priorities around domestic critical minerals supply chains and energy transition technologies have aligned closely with the university's existing research strengths, officials say, contributing to the funding growth.

The increased research funding has supported hiring additional research faculty and graduate student researchers, expanding the university's research capacity across several departments. University officials say this research growth also benefits undergraduate education, since many research grants include funding for undergraduate research assistant positions that give students hands-on research experience.

## Research Funding's Broader Economic Impact

University officials point to research funding's broader economic impact on the surrounding Golden and greater Denver metro area, noting that research grants support not just university jobs but also purchases from regional laboratory equipment suppliers and other research-related vendors. Several university research projects have also led to spin-off companies commercializing university-developed technology, adding another layer of regional economic impact beyond the direct research funding itself.

University officials say they expect continued research funding growth, citing a strong pipeline of pending grant applications in the university's core research strength areas, though they acknowledge that federal research funding priorities can shift with changing federal budget priorities.
