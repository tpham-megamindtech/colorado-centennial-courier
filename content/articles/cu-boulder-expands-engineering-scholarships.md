The University of Colorado Boulder's College of Engineering and Applied Science has expanded its scholarship program, adding new funding aimed at reducing financial barriers for students pursuing degrees in high-demand technical fields like aerospace, computer science, and mechanical engineering.

College officials say the expansion responds directly to rising tuition and living costs that have made engineering degrees increasingly expensive despite strong post-graduation earning potential in the field. "We don't want cost to be the reason a talented student chooses a different major, or a different school entirely," said engineering dean's office spokesperson Rachel Osei.

## Targeting Underrepresented Students in Technical Fields

A significant portion of the new scholarship funding is specifically earmarked for students from backgrounds historically underrepresented in engineering fields, part of a broader university effort to diversify its engineering student population. The college has partnered with several Colorado high schools serving predominantly first-generation college student populations to help identify and recruit eligible students earlier in their academic careers.

Scholarship recipients also gain access to a dedicated mentorship program pairing them with upperclassmen and industry professionals, a support structure college officials say has been important for retention, since financial aid alone doesn't address all the challenges students from underrepresented backgrounds may face in a demanding engineering curriculum.

## Funding Sources and Long-Term Sustainability

The scholarship expansion is funded through a combination of university resources and a fundraising campaign targeting engineering alumni and industry partners, several of whom have significant hiring needs in engineering fields and see scholarship funding as an investment in their future talent pipeline. University officials say they're working to build an endowment specifically for the scholarship program, aiming to make the funding sustainable beyond the initial fundraising campaign.

College officials say they'll evaluate the scholarship program's impact on enrollment diversity and student retention over the next several years, with plans to expand funding further if the program shows measurable success in its stated goals.
