A major Denver health system has invested in AI-assisted diagnostic imaging tools for its radiology department, aiming to improve detection accuracy for certain cancer screenings by flagging areas of concern for radiologists to review alongside their standard image analysis.

Radiology department chair Dr. Owen Bramford emphasizes that the AI tool functions as a supplementary check for radiologists rather than a replacement for radiologist judgment. "This doesn't make the final call on anything," Bramford said. "It's another set of eyes flagging things a radiologist should take a closer look at, which is different from replacing that radiologist's judgment."

## Understanding the Tool's Role in Clinical Workflow

The AI diagnostic tool analyzes imaging studies after a radiologist's initial review, flagging any areas that warrant a second look based on patterns the software has been trained to recognize from analyzing large volumes of historical imaging data. Radiologists then determine whether flagged areas warrant additional follow-up, with the final diagnostic determination remaining entirely within radiologist judgment rather than being automated.

Hospital officials say implementing the tool required significant workflow integration work, ensuring the additional AI-flagged review step didn't meaningfully slow down radiologist throughput given already high imaging study volumes across the department.

## Early Results and Ongoing Evaluation

Early internal data reviewed by the hospital suggests the AI tool has identified a small number of cases where additional follow-up led to earlier cancer detection than might have occurred through standard radiologist review alone, though hospital officials emphasize that this data is preliminary and the tool remains under ongoing evaluation rather than treated as a proven, unqualified success. The hospital is participating in a broader multi-institution study evaluating the tool's effectiveness across different patient populations and imaging types.

Hospital officials say they're proceeding cautiously with any further AI diagnostic tool expansion, emphasizing that patient safety and rigorous clinical evaluation will continue to guide adoption decisions rather than technology enthusiasm alone.
