Felix Okafor launched Front Range Local, a network of hyperlocal news websites covering Colorado communities that had lost dedicated local newspaper coverage in recent years, betting that residents would pay for genuinely local reporting even as many traditional local newspapers have shut down or scaled back significantly.

"There were whole towns where the only local coverage left was whatever made it into a regional paper's roundup story," Okafor said. "That's not the same as having a reporter who actually knows the school board members and shows up to city council meetings." The network now covers six Colorado communities with dedicated local reporters for each site.

## Building a Sustainable Local News Business Model

Okafor has structured Front Range Local around a mix of reader subscriptions and local business advertising, deliberately avoiding dependence on the kind of large-scale programmatic advertising revenue that has struggled to sustain many local news operations elsewhere. Subscription revenue currently represents the larger share of the network's income, a structure Okafor says forces the publication to prioritize content readers actually value enough to pay for.

Each community site operates with a small dedicated staff, typically one or two reporters, supported by shared editing, technology, and business operations functions across the network. Okafor says this structure lets the network achieve some economies of scale while still maintaining genuinely local, dedicated reporting capacity in each community it serves.

## Filling a Gap Left by Local News Decline

Okafor says the network's growth reflects a broader crisis in local news coverage across small and mid-sized communities nationally, with Colorado being no exception to a trend of shrinking local newsroom capacity over the past two decades. He says several of the communities Front Range Local now covers had gone years without any reporter regularly attending local government meetings before the network launched coverage there.

Okafor says he's evaluating expansion into several additional Colorado communities, though he's prioritizing proving the subscription-driven business model is sustainable in the network's current markets before committing to further expansion.
