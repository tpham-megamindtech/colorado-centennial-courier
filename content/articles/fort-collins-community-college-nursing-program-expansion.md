Front Range Community College's Fort Collins campus has expanded the capacity of its nursing program, adding additional enrollment slots and clinical training partnerships in response to persistent nursing shortages affecting regional hospitals and healthcare providers.

Nursing program director Elaine Marsh says the expansion was driven directly by requests from regional healthcare employers who have struggled to fill open nursing positions in recent years. "We were turning away qualified applicants because we simply didn't have enough clinical training slots to expand the program," Marsh said. "Local hospitals have been asking us for years if we could train more nurses."

## Clinical Training Capacity as the Key Constraint

Expanding nursing program enrollment required more than just additional classroom seats, since nursing education depends heavily on hands-on clinical training placements at hospitals and healthcare facilities, a resource that had limited the program's growth in previous years. The college negotiated expanded clinical placement agreements with several regional hospital systems specifically to support the enrollment increase.

The expansion also required hiring additional nursing faculty, a challenge Marsh says is complicated by the fact that experienced nurses qualified to teach can often earn significantly more working directly in clinical healthcare roles than in academic teaching positions. The college has introduced more competitive faculty compensation and flexible scheduling options specifically to address this recruitment challenge.

## Addressing a Regional Healthcare Workforce Crisis

Regional healthcare providers have described the local nursing shortage as a persistent operational challenge, with some facilities reporting extended time to fill open nursing positions and increased reliance on temporary travel nurses at higher cost than permanent staff. Hospital administrators say the expanded local nursing program should help build a more sustainable regional nursing workforce pipeline over time, though most acknowledge that addressing the shortage fully will take several years given how long nursing education takes to complete.

The college says it's already seeing increased application volume following the program expansion announcement, and is evaluating whether further expansion will be possible in future years depending on continued clinical placement capacity.
