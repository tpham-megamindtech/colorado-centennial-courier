Marcus Thorne opened his first boutique, a curated home goods and gift shop, in Vail Village nine years ago after a career in retail merchandising for larger chains. The concept, Alpine Provisions, has since expanded to four locations across Colorado's ski resort towns, including Beaver Creek, Aspen, and Breckenridge.

"I wanted to bring the merchandising discipline I learned at bigger companies to a much smaller, more curated format," Thorne said. Each Alpine Provisions location carries a mix of locally made goods alongside a curated selection of home and lifestyle products, with roughly a third of inventory sourced from Colorado-based makers.

## Curating Inventory for a Tourist Customer Base

Thorne's merchandising strategy is built specifically around the buying patterns of resort town visitors rather than year-round local shoppers, with inventory skewing toward gift-appropriate items and products that evoke the mountain town experience visitors are having during their trip. He says understanding this distinction from standard retail merchandising was key to the concept's early success.

Each location's inventory is also tailored to some degree to its specific town's character, with the Aspen location carrying a higher proportion of premium-priced items compared to the more moderately priced Breckenridge shop, reflecting differences in each town's typical visitor demographics.

## Managing a Highly Seasonal Business Model

Resort town retail carries pronounced seasonal swings between winter ski season, summer tourism, and quieter shoulder seasons, and Thorne says managing cash flow and staffing across these fluctuations has been one of the business's central operational challenges. The company brings on significant seasonal staff for winter and summer peaks while maintaining a smaller core team through shoulder seasons.

Thorne says he's evaluating a potential fifth location, though he's being selective about which additional mountain towns could support the concept, citing lease costs in some of the state's more heavily trafficked resort towns as an increasingly significant constraint on further expansion.
