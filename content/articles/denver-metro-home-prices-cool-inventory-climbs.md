Median home prices across the Denver metro area held roughly flat this quarter, according to figures compiled by regional real estate groups, as the number of active listings climbed to its highest level in several years.

Local agents say the shift marks a meaningful change from the tight, fast-moving market that characterized much of the past decade. "Buyers actually have time to think now," said Renata Colby, a broker who has worked the Denver market for over a dozen years. "That's been rare for a long time here."

## Inventory Growth Outpacing Demand

New listings have grown steadily over the past several quarters, driven partly by homeowners who delayed selling during a period of low mortgage rates now deciding to move as rates have stabilized at a new normal. At the same time, buyer demand has grown more slowly, a mismatch that has started to show up in longer average days-on-market figures across most Denver metro submarkets.

Suburban areas have seen the largest inventory increases, while more central neighborhoods have held price levels more consistently, according to the regional data. Agents attribute the divergence partly to continued demand for shorter commutes and walkable neighborhoods, even as overall market conditions loosen.

## What It Means for Buyers and Sellers

For buyers, the shift has meant more room to negotiate on price and request repairs or concessions, a dynamic that was largely absent during the market's tightest years. Sellers, meanwhile, are adjusting expectations, with several agents reporting more price reductions on listings that have sat longer than sellers initially anticipated.

Economists tracking the regional market caution against reading too much into a single quarter of data, noting that seasonal patterns typically bring a wave of new listings each spring. Still, several local economists say the broader trend toward a more balanced market appears to be holding, rather than reflecting a temporary blip.
