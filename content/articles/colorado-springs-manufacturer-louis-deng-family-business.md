When Louis Deng took over his family's precision components manufacturing business three years ago, the Colorado Springs company was running much the same way it had for the previous three decades under his father's leadership. Deng has since led a significant modernization effort, adding automated production lines while working to preserve the company's longtime workforce.

"My dad built this company on relationships and reliability," Deng said. "I didn't want to walk in and act like none of that mattered just because I'd studied engineering and had new ideas." That balance shaped Deng's approach to introducing automation gradually rather than as a wholesale overhaul.

## Automating Without Displacing Longtime Workers

Rather than replacing existing staff with automated systems, Deng has focused new automation investments on production steps that were previously bottlenecks limiting the company's overall capacity, retraining existing employees to operate and maintain the new equipment rather than reducing headcount. Several employees who had worked manual production lines for over a decade have transitioned into technician roles overseeing the new automated systems.

The transition required significant investment in employee training programs, including partnering with a local community college to develop a customized technical training curriculum specific to the company's new equipment. Deng says this investment in existing staff, rather than hiring new automation specialists from outside, was important both for morale and for retaining institutional knowledge about the company's specific manufacturing processes.

## Balancing Tradition and Growth

The modernization has allowed the company to take on larger, more complex manufacturing contracts that weren't previously feasible with the company's older equipment, contributing to steady revenue growth since Deng took over leadership. He says maintaining the company's reputation for reliability, built over decades under his father's leadership, remains central to how he evaluates whether to pursue new business opportunities.

Deng says his father, now retired, still visits the shop floor occasionally, a continuity he values even as the business has changed significantly under his leadership. "He built the reputation I'm building on," Deng said. "The least I can do is make sure the people who helped him build it are taken care of as we grow."
