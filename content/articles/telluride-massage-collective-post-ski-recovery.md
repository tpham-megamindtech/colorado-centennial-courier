Six independent massage therapists have banded together to open Peak Recovery Collective in Telluride, a shared studio space built specifically around sports and post-activity massage rather than a traditional relaxation-focused spa menu.

Co-founder Marcus Aldenholt says the collective structure lets each therapist keep their own client base and pricing while sharing the overhead of a single studio space. "None of us wanted to run a full spa business," he said. "We wanted to focus on the actual bodywork, and splitting the rent and front-desk costs six ways made that possible."

## A Client Base Built Around Athletes

The collective's client base skews heavily toward skiers, ski patrol staff, and endurance athletes training in the area, a niche Aldenholt says shaped the collective's treatment offerings from the start. Deep tissue and sports massage make up the bulk of bookings, with several therapists also offering specialized modalities like myofascial release and cupping therapy.

Winter remains the collective's busiest season by a wide margin, though Aldenholt says summer trail running and mountain biking activity has helped smooth out what used to be a sharp seasonal drop-off. The studio now runs close to full capacity nearly year-round, a shift from its first year when summer bookings were noticeably thinner.

## Staying Small by Design

Unlike some wellness businesses expanding into multiple locations, Aldenholt says the collective has no plans to grow beyond its current six-therapist structure. "Telluride is small," he said. "We'd rather be the best option for the athletes who are already here than try to scale into something bigger."

The collective has started offering block-booking packages for ski patrol teams and local outfitting companies, giving employees discounted recovery sessions as part of workplace wellness arrangements — a format Aldenholt says has become one of the studio's more reliable revenue sources heading into winter.
