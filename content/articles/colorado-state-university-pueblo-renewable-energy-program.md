Colorado State University Pueblo has launched a new renewable energy technology degree program, directly aligning academic offerings with the Pueblo region's growing clean energy manufacturing sector, which has attracted significant investment in wind turbine component manufacturing over the past several years.

Program director Hector Villareal says the university identified the program as a strategic opportunity given the concentration of clean energy manufacturing employers already operating in the region. "We have major renewable energy manufacturers right here in our backyard," Villareal said. "It made sense to build a program that trains the workforce those companies actually need."

## Aligning Curriculum With Regional Manufacturing Needs

The new program includes both technical coursework in renewable energy systems and hands-on training components developed in consultation with regional clean energy manufacturers, ensuring graduates gain practical skills directly applicable to available regional jobs. Several manufacturing partners have committed to providing internship placements and, in some cases, equipment access for hands-on training components of the program.

University officials say the program was designed to serve both traditional degree-seeking students and working adults already employed in the region's manufacturing sector who want to advance their technical qualifications, offering flexible scheduling options to accommodate both groups of students.

## Supporting a Growing Regional Industry Cluster

Pueblo's renewable energy manufacturing sector has grown significantly over the past several years, with the university program representing part of a broader regional strategy to build a sustainable talent pipeline supporting continued industry growth. Regional economic development officials have expressed strong support for the new program, viewing local workforce training capacity as an important factor companies consider when deciding whether to expand existing regional operations or invest in new facilities elsewhere.

University officials say they're optimistic about the program's growth potential given the strength of regional employer demand, though they note that enrollment growth will depend on continued outreach to ensure prospective students, including those already working in the region's manufacturing sector, are aware of the new program's availability.
