Vail Health has expanded its sports medicine program, adding specialized injury recovery services designed to serve both elite competitive athletes training in the area and the large volume of recreational skiers who sustain injuries during ski season.

Sports medicine director Dr. Renata Kessler says the expansion reflects the dual nature of Vail's patient population, which includes both serious competitive athletes requiring highly specialized rehabilitation and a much larger volume of recreational visitors with more common ski-related injuries like knee ligament tears. "We needed a program flexible enough to serve both populations well," Kessler said. "They have pretty different needs even when the underlying injury looks similar on paper."

## Building Capacity for Ski Season Injury Volume

The expanded program includes additional physical therapy capacity specifically scaled to handle the significant seasonal surge in ski-related injuries during peak winter months, when patient volume for certain injury types can be many times higher than during the rest of the year. Vail Health has structured staffing to flex seasonally, bringing on additional physical therapy and orthopedic staff specifically for the winter season.

The program has also expanded same-day injury evaluation capacity, recognizing that many ski injury patients are visitors on limited-duration trips who need rapid diagnosis and treatment planning rather than being able to accommodate a longer, more spread-out evaluation and treatment timeline typical of local patients.

## Serving Elite Athletes Alongside Recreational Visitors

Beyond recreational ski injuries, the program's elite athlete services support competitive skiers and other athletes training in the Vail area, offering specialized rehabilitation protocols designed for athletes needing to return to competitive performance levels rather than simply resuming normal daily activities. Kessler says maintaining expertise across both patient populations requires a broader range of physical therapy and orthopedic specialization than a program serving either population exclusively would need.

Vail Health says it's evaluating additional program expansion based on continued growth in both winter sports tourism and the area's profile as a training location for competitive skiing and other mountain sports.
