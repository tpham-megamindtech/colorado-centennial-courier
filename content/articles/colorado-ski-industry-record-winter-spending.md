Colorado's ski industry is on pace for a record winter season by total visitor spending, according to preliminary figures from a statewide tourism association, driven by strong lift ticket sales, lodging demand, and consistent early-season snowfall across the state's major resorts.

Industry representatives attribute the strong season partly to favorable weather timing, with several resorts reporting above-average natural snowfall arriving earlier than in recent seasons, allowing for extended operating calendars. "An early, reliable start to the season makes a real difference in total visitor days," said tourism association spokesperson Delia Marsh.

## Lodging and Retail Benefit Alongside Resorts

The spending increase extends well beyond lift ticket revenue, with mountain town lodging occupancy and retail sales also running ahead of prior-year figures, according to the association's data. Restaurants and retail shops in resort communities report some of their strongest seasons in recent memory, with several business owners citing both higher visitor volume and increased average spending per visitor.

International visitors have also returned in larger numbers this season, according to resort marketing data, a trend industry officials attribute partly to favorable currency exchange conditions making Colorado ski destinations relatively more affordable for some overseas travelers.

## Watching Long-Term Climate Considerations

Even amid the strong season, industry groups continue to invest in snowmaking infrastructure and long-term climate adaptation planning, acknowledging that a single strong season doesn't offset broader concerns about long-term snowpack reliability. Several resorts have expanded snowmaking capacity in recent years specifically to reduce reliance on natural snowfall timing.

Tourism officials say the strong season is likely to support continued investment in resort infrastructure and mountain town hospitality capacity, though they note that next season's performance will depend heavily on weather patterns that remain difficult to predict this far in advance.
