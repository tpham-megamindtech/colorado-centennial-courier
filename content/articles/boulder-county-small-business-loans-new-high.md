Small business lending volume in Boulder County reached a new high this past year, according to figures compiled by a regional economic development office, driven largely by demand from local retail and hospitality businesses looking to expand or renovate.

Community bank and credit union lenders in the area say demand has been particularly strong among businesses seeking capital for physical space improvements, rather than the working capital loans that dominated lending activity during leaner years. "We're seeing more expansion loans and fewer survival loans," said Tomas Whitfield, a commercial lending officer at a Boulder-based community bank.

## Retail and Hospitality Lead Demand

Restaurants, retail shops, and small hospitality businesses account for the largest share of new lending activity, according to the development office's figures, with many borrowers citing plans to renovate existing space or open additional locations within the county. Several lenders note that loan approval rates have remained relatively steady even as volume has grown, suggesting the increase reflects genuine demand rather than loosened lending standards.

Interest rates on small business loans have remained a point of concern for some borrowers, with several business owners noting that debt service costs still weigh on expansion decisions even as they move forward with financing. Lenders say they've seen more borrowers opting for shorter loan terms to manage overall interest costs.

## A Broader Economic Signal

Economic development officials view the lending growth as a positive signal about local business confidence, though they caution that lending volume alone doesn't capture the full health of the small business sector. Some officials note that rising commercial rents in parts of Boulder County have offset some of the benefit of increased loan availability for businesses considering expansion.

Local business associations are pushing for continued state and county support for small business lending programs, arguing that access to affordable capital remains a key constraint for smaller operators compared to well-capitalized regional chains.
