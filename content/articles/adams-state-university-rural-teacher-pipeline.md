Adams State University in Alamosa has expanded its rural teacher preparation program, adding scholarship funding and expanded student teaching placements specifically aimed at training and retaining teachers for Colorado's rural school districts, many of which face persistent teacher staffing shortages.

Program director Beatrice Fontaine says rural school districts often struggle to attract and retain teachers compared to urban and suburban districts, a gap the university's rural-focused teacher preparation program specifically aims to address by recruiting and training teacher candidates already connected to rural communities. "Teachers who grew up in rural areas are more likely to stay in rural areas," Fontaine said. "That's the core insight behind this whole program."

## Recruiting From Within Rural Communities

The program prioritizes recruiting teacher candidates who already have ties to rural Colorado communities, on the theory that these candidates are more likely to return to and remain in rural teaching positions after graduation compared to candidates without existing community ties. Scholarship funding specifically targets students committing to teach in rural districts for a set number of years following graduation, a structure designed to directly address rural teacher retention rather than just initial placement.

The expanded program also includes dedicated student teaching placements in partner rural school districts, giving teacher candidates direct experience with the specific challenges and rewards of rural teaching before they commit to a full-time rural teaching position after graduation.

## Addressing a Statewide Rural Staffing Challenge

Rural school districts across Colorado have reported persistent difficulty filling teaching positions, particularly in specialized subject areas like science and special education, according to state education department data. Adams State officials say the expanded rural teacher pipeline program represents one of several statewide efforts attempting to address this challenge, though they acknowledge that rural teacher shortages are a complex problem unlikely to be solved by any single program alone.

University officials say they're tracking graduate placement and retention data specifically for rural teaching positions, viewing sustained rural teacher retention, rather than just initial job placement, as the true measure of the program's long-term success.
