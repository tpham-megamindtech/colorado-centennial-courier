Colorado's state Medicaid program has expanded coverage options specifically targeting rural counties, adding provisions aimed at addressing historically limited healthcare provider networks that have made it difficult for some rural Medicaid recipients to access covered care close to home.

State Medicaid office officials say the expansion includes provisions specifically allowing broader use of telehealth services under Medicaid coverage in designated rural counties, along with increased reimbursement rates for rural providers accepting Medicaid patients, an adjustment aimed at addressing rural provider shortages that have limited network adequacy in some areas. "Having Medicaid coverage on paper doesn't help if there's no provider within a reasonable distance who accepts it," said Medicaid program director Estelle Whitfield.

## Addressing Network Adequacy Gaps in Rural Areas

State officials say network adequacy, meaning whether Medicaid recipients can actually access covered services within a reasonable distance and wait time, has been a persistent challenge in rural counties compared to the state's urban and suburban areas. The expanded telehealth provisions and increased rural reimbursement rates are both aimed at addressing this specific gap, rather than expanding who qualifies for Medicaid coverage itself.

Rural healthcare providers have generally welcomed the increased reimbursement rates, though some provider groups say reimbursement increases alone won't fully resolve rural network adequacy challenges given broader rural healthcare workforce shortages that limit how many providers are available to serve rural Medicaid patients regardless of reimbursement rates.

## Evaluating the Expansion's Early Impact

State officials say they're tracking rural Medicaid recipient access metrics closely following the expansion, including appointment wait times and the geographic distance recipients travel to access covered care, viewing improvement in these specific metrics as the clearest measure of whether the expansion is achieving its intended goals. Early data reviewed by the state Medicaid office suggests modest improvement in rural network adequacy metrics since the expansion took effect, though officials caution that fully evaluating the expansion's impact will require a longer period of data collection.

State officials say further Medicaid program adjustments targeting rural access gaps remain under consideration, depending on how effectively the current expansion addresses the network adequacy challenges it was designed to target.
