Durango School District 9-R has invested in a new career-technical education facility, expanding hands-on training options in fields including automotive technology, culinary arts, and construction trades for high school students not necessarily pursuing a traditional four-year college degree.

Career and technical education director Rosalind Chavez says the new facility responds to growing student and parent interest in practical skills training that leads directly to employable credentials, alongside continued academic coursework. "Not every student's best path is a four-year university," Chavez said. "We wanted a facility that treats these other paths with the same seriousness and investment."

## Equipping the Facility for Real-World Training

The new facility includes updated equipment donated or discounted by local employers in the represented trade fields, ensuring students train on equipment similar to what they'll actually encounter in regional workplaces after graduation. District officials say building these employer equipment partnerships took considerable relationship-building, since many local businesses had limited previous experience donating equipment or expertise to public school programs.

Beyond equipment, several local employers have also committed to providing guest instruction and internship opportunities for students in the career-technical programs, giving students direct exposure to potential future employers before they even graduate high school.

## Addressing Regional Workforce Needs

Durango-area employers in fields like automotive repair and skilled construction trades have reported ongoing difficulty finding qualified entry-level workers, a challenge district officials say the expanded career-technical facility is directly intended to help address over time. District officials emphasize that the facility serves both students planning to enter the workforce directly after high school and those planning to pursue further technical certification or community college training in these fields.

District officials say early enrollment interest in the expanded programs has been strong, and they're evaluating whether additional investment in equipment or instructor capacity will be needed to meet continued student demand for the career-technical facility's programs.
