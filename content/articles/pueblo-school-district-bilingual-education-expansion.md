Pueblo School District has expanded its dual-language bilingual education program to additional elementary schools this year, responding to a growing bilingual student population and research showing strong academic outcomes for well-implemented dual-language programs.

Bilingual education director Marisela Trujillo says the district's existing dual-language programs, which teach core academic subjects in both English and Spanish, had waitlists at several schools, prompting the expansion to additional campuses. "Families wanted this option and we simply didn't have enough seats," Trujillo said. "Expanding felt like the obvious next step."

## Recruiting Bilingual Teaching Staff

Expanding the program required recruiting additional bilingual teachers qualified to deliver academic instruction in both English and Spanish, a specialized hiring challenge given competition among districts statewide for a limited pool of qualified bilingual educators. The district has partnered with regional teacher preparation programs to help build a stronger local bilingual teacher pipeline for future hiring needs.

The district has also invested in bilingual instructional materials and professional development training for teachers new to dual-language instruction, ensuring that both new and existing bilingual program schools maintain consistent instructional quality as the program expands across more campuses.

## Building Support Among Non-Bilingual Families

District officials say they've worked to communicate the benefits of dual-language education to non-Spanish-speaking families as well, since some dual-language program models include native English speakers in classrooms alongside native Spanish speakers, with both groups gaining bilingual proficiency as an outcome. Research on well-implemented dual-language programs has generally shown strong academic outcomes for both groups of students, information the district has shared in community meetings introducing the program to additional school communities.

District officials say they're evaluating further expansion to additional schools based on continued family demand, though they note that program quality depends heavily on adequate bilingual staffing, which will continue to shape how quickly the district can responsibly grow the program further.
