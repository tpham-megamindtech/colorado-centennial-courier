The University of Colorado Colorado Springs has added a dedicated aerospace engineering track within its broader engineering program, directly responding to the region's dense concentration of aerospace and defense employers who have expressed strong interest in hiring locally trained aerospace engineers.

Engineering department chair Felipe Ordonez says the university had long offered aerospace-adjacent coursework within its general mechanical engineering program, but regional employer feedback consistently pointed to demand for a more dedicated, clearly identified aerospace engineering credential. "Employers wanted graduates with the aerospace engineering title specifically," Ordonez said. "The coursework existed, but it needed to be its own recognized track."

## Building Curriculum Around Regional Employer Needs

The new aerospace engineering track was developed with direct input from several regional aerospace and defense employers, who helped identify specific technical competencies and even suggested specialized coursework topics aligned with current industry needs. Several of these employer partners have also committed to offering internship placements specifically for students in the new track, providing direct pathways into regional aerospace careers.

University officials say the track required hiring additional specialized faculty with direct aerospace industry experience, a recruitment effort that took advantage of the region's existing concentration of aerospace professionals, some of whom were interested in transitioning into academic teaching roles later in their careers.

## Positioning Graduates for Regional Employment

University officials emphasize that the aerospace engineering track is explicitly designed with regional employment in mind, given the concentration of aerospace and defense employers in the Colorado Springs area specifically. Early student interest in the new track has exceeded initial enrollment projections, according to university officials, who attribute the strong interest partly to the track's clear connection to well-paying regional career opportunities.

University officials say they're monitoring graduate employment outcomes closely as the first cohort progresses through the program, viewing strong regional employment placement as the clearest measure of whether the new track is successfully meeting its intended goals.
