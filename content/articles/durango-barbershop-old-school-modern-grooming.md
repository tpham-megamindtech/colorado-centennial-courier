Iron Horse Barbershop opened on Main Avenue in Durango this winter, built around a straightforward pitch: traditional straight-razor shaves and classic cuts, paired with a retail counter stocked with modern grooming products.

Owner Casey Marlowe, a longtime barber who trained in Denver before moving back to southwest Colorado, says the shop's format was a deliberate response to what he saw as a gap in the local market. "There wasn't really a full-service barbershop here that took the craft seriously," he said. "Just a couple of quick-cut chains and home haircuts."

## Building a Retail Line Alongside the Chair

Beyond haircuts and shaves, Iron Horse has built out a retail section carrying beard oils, pomades, and skincare products from small independent grooming brands, a category Marlowe says has grown into a meaningful part of the shop's revenue faster than he expected. He estimates retail sales now account for roughly 15 percent of total revenue, a figure he's aiming to grow through in-shop product demonstrations during appointments.

The shop has also started offering appointment-based grooming packages that bundle a haircut, beard trim, and hot towel shave at a set price, a structure Marlowe says appeals to clients who want a complete service without negotiating add-ons at checkout.

## Serving a Growing Local Market

Durango's population has grown steadily in recent years, and Marlowe says that growth has brought in newer residents accustomed to full-service barbershops from larger cities. "A lot of our clients moved here from Denver or out of state, and they had a shop like this back home," he said. "We're just trying to give people here that same option."

Marlowe is already planning to add a second barber chair by early summer, citing a waitlist that has built up faster than he anticipated for weekend appointment slots.
