Yampa Valley Medical Center has opened a second urgent care location in Steamboat Springs, addressing significant seasonal demand spikes tied to the area's tourism-driven population swings during peak winter ski season and summer tourist months.

Urgent care services director Bret Halloway says the area's year-round resident population doesn't come close to reflecting actual healthcare demand during peak tourist seasons, when the town's effective population can multiply several times over with visitors. "Our single urgent care location was getting overwhelmed during peak weeks," Halloway said. "We needed real additional capacity, not just longer hours at the existing location."

## Sizing Capacity for Extreme Seasonal Swings

Unlike most healthcare facilities that plan capacity around relatively stable year-round demand, Yampa Valley Medical Center's urgent care planning must account for demand that can vary dramatically between off-season periods and peak tourist weeks, a planning challenge Halloway says is unique to resort communities like Steamboat Springs. The new location allows the medical center to flex staffing across both locations based on seasonal demand, rather than being constrained by a single location's maximum capacity during peak periods.

The new location was specifically sited near the ski resort base area, reflecting data showing a significant share of urgent care visits during winter months are ski and snowboard-related injuries among visitors staying near the resort rather than local residents.

## Balancing Tourist and Resident Healthcare Needs

Halloway says a key design consideration for the new location was ensuring it serves both visiting tourists needing urgent injury care and local residents with more routine urgent care needs, without one population's needs crowding out the other during peak demand periods. The medical center has implemented triage protocols designed to prioritize based on medical urgency rather than resident or visitor status, though Halloway acknowledges balancing capacity for both populations remains an ongoing operational challenge during the busiest weeks of ski season.

The medical center says it's evaluating whether additional seasonal staffing adjustments will be needed based on the new location's first full ski season, with further changes possible depending on how effectively the expanded capacity manages peak season demand.
