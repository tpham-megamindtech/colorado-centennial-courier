Boulder Valley School District has introduced a formal outdoor education curriculum across several elementary and middle schools, integrating environmental science instruction with regular hands-on outdoor experiences at nearby open space and trail areas.

Curriculum coordinator Ines Halvorsen says the program formalizes outdoor learning practices that individual teachers had informally incorporated into their classrooms for years, building a consistent district-wide curriculum framework rather than leaving outdoor education dependent on individual teacher initiative. "We had wonderful teachers doing this on their own for a long time," Halvorsen said. "We wanted to make sure every student got access to it, not just students lucky enough to have a particular teacher."

## Structuring Outdoor Learning Into the School Day

The new curriculum designates regular weekly blocks for outdoor instruction, tied directly to science curriculum standards rather than treated as a separate enrichment activity outside core academic requirements. Teachers received training on leading outdoor lessons safely and effectively, including how to manage a classroom in an outdoor setting where the usual classroom structure doesn't directly apply.

The district worked with local open space agencies to establish formal access agreements for schools located near trail systems and natural areas, ensuring consistent, reliable access to outdoor learning sites rather than relying on informal arrangements that could change unpredictably.

## Measuring the Program's Educational Impact

District officials say they're tracking student engagement and science curriculum performance data to evaluate whether the formalized outdoor education approach produces measurable academic benefits compared to traditional indoor-only science instruction. Early informal feedback from participating teachers has been positive, with several reporting improved student engagement during outdoor lessons compared to comparable indoor classroom sessions.

The district says it's considering expanding the outdoor education curriculum to additional grade levels based on the initial rollout's reception, though officials note that scaling the program further will require additional teacher training and continued coordination with local land management agencies to ensure adequate outdoor access capacity.
