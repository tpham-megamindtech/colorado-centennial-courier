Grace Whitfield spent twelve years guiding backcountry skiing and mountaineering trips across southwest Colorado before launching Pass Line Gear, a direct-to-consumer outdoor apparel label built around products she designed to fill gaps she encountered repeatedly while guiding.

"Every piece we make started as something I couldn't find on the market and just built myself out of necessity," Whitfield said. The company's first product, a modular layering system designed for rapidly changing mountain weather, grew out of prototypes Whitfield sewed herself for her own guiding trips.

## Designing From Direct Field Experience

Whitfield's approach to product development remains rooted in her guiding background, with new designs typically tested during actual backcountry trips before being finalized for production. She still guides a handful of trips each season, partly for the income and partly, she says, to stay connected to the actual conditions her products need to perform in.

This field-first design approach has become a core part of the brand's marketing, with product pages featuring detailed accounts of the specific trips and conditions that shaped each design decision. Whitfield says the approach has resonated particularly well with serious backcountry users skeptical of gear designed primarily by marketing teams rather than practitioners.

## Growing a Niche Direct-to-Consumer Brand

Pass Line Gear sells exclusively through its own website rather than wholesale retail partners, a structure Whitfield says preserves higher margins despite requiring the company to build its own customer acquisition channels from scratch. Growth has come primarily through word of mouth within backcountry skiing and mountaineering communities, supplemented by a modest social media presence built around trip reports and gear testing content.

Whitfield says she's resisted pressure from potential investors to pursue more aggressive growth, preferring to keep the company small and focused on a core group of serious backcountry users rather than expanding into broader, more casual outdoor recreation markets.
