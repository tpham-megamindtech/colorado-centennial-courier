Oil and gas employment in the Grand Junction area is showing modest recovery after a prolonged downturn, according to regional workforce data, though total jobs in the sector remain well below the peaks reached over a decade ago.

Regional economic officials attribute the modest uptick to a combination of stabilized commodity prices and renewed drilling activity in parts of the Western Slope, though they caution that the recovery has been gradual rather than a sharp rebound. "We're not talking about a boom," said regional economic analyst Frank Delgado. "It's a slow climb back from a pretty deep hole."

## A Sector Still Adjusting to Structural Change

Beyond price cycles, the sector has undergone structural changes in recent years, including automation that has reduced the number of workers needed per well and continued diversification of the regional economy away from historic reliance on oil and gas employment. Local officials note that even as drilling activity has picked up modestly, job growth in the sector has lagged production growth due to these efficiency changes.

Workforce development programs in the region have shifted focus toward diversifying the local economy, including growth in outdoor recreation, agriculture, and healthcare employment, rather than betting on a full return to historic oil and gas employment levels.

## Balancing Recovery With Economic Diversification

Local officials describe a deliberate strategy of welcoming the oil and gas sector's modest recovery while continuing to invest in other economic sectors, rather than returning to a heavy reliance on a single volatile industry. "We got burned by that dependence before," Delgado said. "Nobody wants to rebuild the whole local economy on one commodity cycle again."

Regional economic development plans continue to emphasize diversification, even as officials acknowledge the oil and gas sector's continued importance to the area's tax base and remains a meaningful, if smaller, part of the regional employment picture.
