Colorado's state housing authority has expanded funding for its first-time homebuyer down payment assistance program, adding resources after demand consistently outpaced available funding in recent application cycles.

The program provides low-interest, deferred-payment loans that first-time buyers can use toward down payments and closing costs, targeted at households earning below a set income threshold relative to the local area's median income. Housing authority officials say application volume has grown steadily as home prices across the state have continued to outpace income growth for many prospective buyers.

## Demand Consistently Exceeds Funding

Previous funding cycles have run out of available assistance funds well before the application period closed, according to housing authority data, leaving qualified applicants unable to access the program in a given cycle. "We've had to turn away qualified buyers simply because the funding ran out first," said housing authority program director Elise Marchetti. "This expansion is meant to close that gap, at least partially."

The expanded funding is expected to support several hundred additional buyers over the coming year, though officials acknowledge that demand may still outpace the increased funding level given current home price trends across the state.

## Regional Disparities in Program Reach

The program's reach varies significantly across the state, with mountain resort communities and the Denver metro area presenting particular affordability challenges given local home prices relative to the area median income thresholds used to determine eligibility. Housing authority officials say they're evaluating whether regional adjustments to program parameters could better address these local affordability disparities.

Housing advocates have welcomed the funding expansion while continuing to push for broader state investment in affordable housing production, arguing that down payment assistance alone doesn't address underlying housing supply constraints driving price growth statewide.
