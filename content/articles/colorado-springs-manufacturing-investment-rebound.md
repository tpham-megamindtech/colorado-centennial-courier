Colorado Springs is seeing renewed manufacturing investment after a quieter period following the pandemic, with several companies in aerospace-adjacent and precision manufacturing sectors announcing facility expansions over the past year.

Regional economic development officials point to a combination of factors driving the rebound, including proximity to the area's established aerospace and defense supply chain, comparatively lower industrial real estate costs than the Denver metro area, and state tax incentives aimed at manufacturing investment.

## Aerospace Supply Chain Draws New Investment

Several of the recent facility announcements come from companies supplying components to larger aerospace and defense contractors already established in the region, a pattern local officials describe as a multiplier effect from the area's existing industrial base. "Once you have a critical mass of aerospace manufacturing, suppliers want to be close," said Denise Whitcombe, an economic development official tracking the trend.

The expansions have brought hundreds of new manufacturing jobs to the region over the past year, according to figures compiled by local development authorities, with several companies citing workforce availability alongside cost factors in choosing Colorado Springs over other potential sites.

## Workforce Training Becomes a Priority

The investment rebound has put new pressure on local workforce training programs to keep pace with manufacturing hiring needs. Community colleges in the region have expanded machining and precision manufacturing certificate programs in response, working directly with several of the expanding companies to align curriculum with specific technical skill needs.

Officials caution that sustained growth will depend on continued workforce pipeline development, noting that several companies have cited skilled labor availability as a more significant constraint on further expansion than real estate or incentive availability.
