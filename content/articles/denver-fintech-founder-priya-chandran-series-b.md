Priya Chandran has closed a Series B funding round for her Denver-based fintech company, Ledgerline, marking one of the largest venture raises by a Colorado startup so far this year and a significant milestone for a company Chandran started in her apartment four years ago.

Ledgerline builds lending software that helps community banks and credit unions process small business loan applications faster, a niche Chandran identified while working as a loan officer earlier in her career. "I spent years watching good small business owners get stuck in weeks-long approval processes because the underlying systems were so outdated," she said. "I knew there had to be a better way to do this."

## From Loan Officer to Startup Founder

Chandran's background gives her an unusual level of domain expertise for a fintech founder, having spent nearly a decade in community banking before starting Ledgerline. She says that experience shaped the company's early product decisions, particularly around building software that works within existing bank compliance frameworks rather than trying to bypass them.

The company's first customers were small community banks and credit unions in Colorado, a deliberate choice Chandran says let her refine the product with institutions she already had relationships with before expanding to a broader national customer base. Ledgerline now serves lending institutions across a dozen states, with Colorado financial institutions still representing a meaningful share of its customer base.

## Using the New Funding to Scale

The new capital will primarily fund engineering hires and an expanded sales team, according to Chandran, as the company looks to compete for larger lending institution clients that require more extensive compliance and integration capabilities. She says the company has been deliberate about growing headcount at a sustainable pace rather than scaling as fast as possible immediately after funding rounds.

Chandran, one of relatively few women leading a venture-backed fintech company in the region, says she's also begun mentoring other Colorado fintech founders informally, citing a lack of that kind of support when she was first starting the company as a gap she wants to help close for others.
