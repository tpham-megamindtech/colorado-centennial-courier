A round of layoffs among several mid-sized technology employers along the Front Range is starting to show measurable effects on local consumer spending, according to regional economists tracking the sector.

The layoffs, spread across software and hardware companies based in the Denver and Boulder areas, have affected several thousand workers over recent months. While the region's overall unemployment rate remains comparatively low, economists say the concentrated nature of the job losses within a single high-paying sector has an outsized effect on discretionary spending in the areas where those workers live.

## Tracking the Downstream Effects

Local restaurant and retail associations in neighborhoods with high concentrations of tech workers have reported softer sales over the past two quarters, a pattern several business owners attribute directly to the layoffs. "We felt it before the headlines caught up," said Owen Marsh, who owns a restaurant in a Boulder neighborhood popular with tech employees. "Reservations for weekday lunch dropped off first."

Economists caution that isolating the layoffs' precise economic impact is difficult, since the Front Range economy remains diversified across healthcare, aerospace, and other sectors that have continued adding jobs during the same period. Still, several regional economic forecasts have been revised slightly downward to reflect softer near-term consumer spending expectations.

## A Sector Adjusting After Rapid Growth

The layoffs follow a period of rapid hiring across the region's tech sector, and several affected companies have cited broader industry-wide cost-cutting rather than Colorado-specific factors as the primary driver. Some laid-off workers have found new positions relatively quickly given the region's overall demand for technical talent, though local workforce development groups report longer average job searches than in previous years.

State economic development officials say they're watching the situation closely but have not yet seen evidence of a broader economic slowdown extending beyond the tech sector itself.
