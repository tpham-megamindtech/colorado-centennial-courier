Colorado Mesa University in Grand Junction has grown overall enrollment by expanding its online and hybrid degree program offerings, reaching students across the rural Western Slope region who may not have been able to relocate to campus for a traditional in-person degree program.

University enrollment officials say the online expansion has been particularly effective at reaching working adults and parents balancing family responsibilities who wouldn't otherwise have pursued a degree requiring full-time campus attendance. "A lot of our new online students already live in our service region," said enrollment director Talia Marsh. "They just needed a format that actually fit their lives."

## Balancing Online Growth With Campus Identity

University officials say a key challenge in expanding online offerings has been maintaining the same academic quality and level of student support that characterizes the university's traditional in-person programs, rather than treating online education as a lower-cost, lower-touch alternative. The university has invested in dedicated online student advising staff and expanded virtual tutoring services specifically to address this concern.

Faculty teaching online courses have also received additional training in online pedagogy, a deliberate investment university officials say was necessary since effective online teaching requires different techniques than simply recording in-person lectures. Several faculty members who initially expressed skepticism about online teaching have since become strong advocates after seeing engagement levels in well-designed online courses.

## Serving a Historically Underserved Rural Region

Colorado Mesa's Western Slope service region includes many rural communities with limited access to four-year degree programs, a gap enrollment officials say the university's online expansion has directly addressed. The university has also partnered with several rural high schools to offer dual-enrollment online courses, giving high school students an early introduction to the university's programs and instructors.

University officials say continued enrollment growth will depend on further expanding online program offerings into additional degree areas, based on demand data from prospective students in the university's broader rural service region.
