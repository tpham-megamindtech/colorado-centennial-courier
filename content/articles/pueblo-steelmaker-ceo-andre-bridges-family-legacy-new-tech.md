Andre Bridges represents the third generation of his family to lead Bridges Steel Works, a Pueblo manufacturing company his grandfather founded shortly after returning from military service decades ago. Under Andre's leadership, the company has invested heavily in new manufacturing technology while maintaining the specialized steel products that have defined the business for generations.

"My grandfather built this company on a handshake and a reputation for quality," Bridges said. "My job is making sure that same reputation survives modern manufacturing competition, which means we can't just keep doing things exactly the way he did them."

## Investing in Technology While Honoring Tradition

Bridges has introduced computer-modeling software that allows the company to design and test custom steel components digitally before physical production, reducing costly trial-and-error prototyping that previously extended project timelines significantly. The investment required hiring the company's first dedicated engineering staff, a departure from the company's historically craft-based approach to custom manufacturing work.

Despite the technology investment, Bridges says the company has maintained its focus on custom, specialized steel products rather than pursuing higher-volume commodity manufacturing that would compete more directly on price against larger, more automated steel producers. "We've never been the cheapest option," he said. "We've been the option for clients who need something nobody else can make correctly."

## Passing Down Institutional Knowledge

With several longtime employees nearing retirement, Bridges has prioritized documenting institutional knowledge that previously existed only in senior employees' heads, pairing experienced staff with younger hires specifically to transfer decades of accumulated manufacturing expertise before that knowledge is lost to retirement.

Bridges says he's uncertain whether a fourth generation of his family will eventually lead the company, noting that his own children haven't expressed interest in manufacturing careers so far. In the meantime, he's focused on building management structures robust enough that the company's success doesn't depend entirely on family leadership continuing indefinitely.
