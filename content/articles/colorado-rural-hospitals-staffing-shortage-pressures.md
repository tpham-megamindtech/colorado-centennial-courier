Colorado's rural hospitals continue to face significant staffing shortages across multiple clinical roles, according to a recent state healthcare workforce report, prompting new state initiatives aimed at supporting rural healthcare recruitment and retention efforts.

The report found that rural hospitals across the state report longer average time to fill open nursing and physician positions compared to urban and suburban hospitals, a gap that has persisted despite various recruitment efforts over recent years. "Rural hospitals are competing for the same limited pool of healthcare workers as everyone else, but with fewer resources and a harder recruiting pitch in some cases," said state rural health office director Priscilla Anders.

## Understanding Why Rural Recruitment Is Especially Difficult

Rural hospital administrators cite several factors making recruitment particularly challenging, including limited spousal employment opportunities in small rural communities, fewer amenities that some healthcare workers prioritize when choosing where to live, and generally lower compensation than urban hospitals can offer given rural hospitals' more constrained budgets. Several rural hospital administrators say these factors compound each other, making recruitment significantly harder than simply offering competitive wages could solve alone.

Some rural hospitals have found success recruiting healthcare workers with existing ties to the community, similar to strategies rural school districts have used for teacher recruitment, though administrators acknowledge this approach alone can't fill the full scope of current staffing gaps.

## New State Initiatives Aim to Support Rural Recruitment

The state has introduced new loan forgiveness programs specifically targeting healthcare workers who commit to rural practice for a set number of years, alongside grant funding to help rural hospitals improve housing options for recruited staff, addressing one of the specific barriers rural hospital administrators identified. State officials say these initiatives are informed directly by rural hospital administrator feedback about the specific barriers their recruitment efforts face.

State health officials say addressing rural healthcare staffing shortages will likely require sustained, multi-year investment rather than a single initiative, given how deeply rooted the underlying recruitment challenges are within broader rural economic and demographic trends.
