Little Peaks Preschool Network, which operates several early childhood education centers across Boulder County, has expanded subsidized enrollment slots for working families, addressing a persistent shortage of affordable early childhood education options in a county where childcare costs have risen significantly faster than typical family incomes.

Network director Josephine Marsh says the subsidized slots are funded through a combination of county early childhood initiative grants and a sliding-scale tuition model that allows higher-income families to effectively subsidize reduced tuition rates for lower-income families within the same centers. "We didn't want to create a separate, lower-quality track for subsidized families," Marsh said. "Every child in our centers gets the same program, regardless of what their family is paying."

## Addressing a Regional Childcare Affordability Crisis

Boulder County childcare costs have risen substantially in recent years, driven partly by the county's overall high cost of living and partly by increased childcare staffing costs as the sector has struggled with worker recruitment and retention challenges common across the early childhood education industry nationally. Family advocacy groups have identified childcare affordability as one of the most significant financial pressures facing working families with young children in the county.

The expanded subsidized enrollment specifically targets working families who earn too much to qualify for state childcare assistance programs but still find market-rate childcare costs to be a significant financial burden, a gap network officials say existing state assistance programs don't adequately address.

## Balancing Financial Sustainability With Access Goals

Network officials say balancing expanded subsidized access with the financial sustainability needed to maintain quality staffing and facilities has required careful financial planning, including securing multi-year grant commitments rather than relying on funding that could disappear after a single budget cycle. Marsh says the network is being deliberate about how many subsidized slots it can sustainably offer, wanting to avoid a situation where expanded access commitments outpace available funding in future years.

The network says it's evaluating whether the sliding-scale funding model could be extended to additional centers within the network based on the success of the initial expansion, though any further growth will depend on securing additional grant funding commitments.
