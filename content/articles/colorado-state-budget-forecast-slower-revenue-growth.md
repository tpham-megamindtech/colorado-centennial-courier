Colorado's latest quarterly budget forecast points to noticeably slower state tax revenue growth than earlier projections, a shift that has prompted lawmakers to revisit spending plans ahead of the next legislative session.

State budget officials attribute the slowdown to a combination of factors, including softer income tax withholding growth and a moderation in retail sales tax collections compared to the stronger growth seen in recent years. The forecast doesn't project a revenue decline, but the reduced growth rate is significant enough that several budget committee members have said it will require adjustments to planned spending increases.

## Lawmakers Reassess Spending Priorities

Legislative budget leaders say the forecast will likely mean scaled-back increases for several discretionary spending categories, though officials have emphasized that core funding commitments for education and healthcare programs are expected to remain largely intact. "This isn't a crisis forecast," said state budget committee chair Miriam Solberg. "But it does mean we can't fund everything at the pace some had hoped."

The state's constitutional revenue limits add another layer of complexity to budget planning, with officials noting that slower revenue growth interacts differently with those limits than a revenue decline would. Budget analysts are working through the specific implications ahead of the next forecast update.

## Watching Consumer Spending Trends Closely

Economists advising the state's budget office point to moderating consumer spending as a key factor behind the softer sales tax collections, tying the trend partly to the same regional economic pressures affecting tech sector employment along the Front Range. Officials say they'll have a clearer picture of whether the slowdown is a temporary adjustment or a more sustained trend once the next quarterly forecast is released.

Lawmakers are expected to debate specific budget allocations in the coming months, with the revised revenue projections serving as a baseline for those negotiations.
